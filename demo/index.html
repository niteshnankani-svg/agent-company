A support ticket, one department at a time

Every ticket is classified by a keyword orchestrator, handed to the one department that owns it, and — on the Backend / Ticketing lane — triaged by a fine-tuned DistilBERT model that assigns a support team or, when the call is close or sensitive, routes to a human. How it is built & what was fixed

Architecture

Orchestrator — a transparent keyword classifier over ~45 IT terms plus finance / HR / legal / export vocab. A single hit clears the 0.75 confidence bar and routes locally; only genuinely ambiguous text falls through to an LLM.

Backend / Ticketing — PII is scrubbed and prompt-injection / security phrases are caught before the model runs; then DistilBERT returns a 5-way distribution. Access and Security always go to a human; a confident benign class (≥ 0.65) is routed to its team; anything under the model's 0.55 accept gate is called uncertain and escalated.

Verifier + graph — the decision is checked for internal consistency; on failure the ticket retries its own department up to 3 times, then hard-escalates. The model classifies — it never marks a ticket resolved.

The regression this fixes

Two bugs compounded so that every ticket landed in one bucket, all needing a human:

  • backend_head hard-coded decision = human_escalation for all tickets — a 0.97 "replace this keyboard" was treated like a breach.
    Now: route_to_team:<team> for confident, benign classes.
  • The orchestrator knew only 5 IT keywords, so real tickets fell to an LLM that lumped everything into backend.
    Now: ~45 terms, deterministic and offline.
  • Every verifier retry jumped to finance_head regardless of department.
    Now: retries the department that ran, fails closed otherwise.
